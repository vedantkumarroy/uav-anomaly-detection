Q6: Are the 10^13 values sentinels or division-by-zero?

Answer: Neither. They are the squared normalized innovation used by PX4's EKF2:

    innovation_test_ratio = innovation^2 / (gate_size^2 * innovation_variance)

Verified by reconstructing the ratio from `estimator_innovations` and
`estimator_innovation_variances` on log 37f5014b-c8fc-447d-9974-f72adcd6ff34:

  sample 12: innovation=-1.3492e7, variance=2.0928
    reconstructed: (1.3492e7)^2 / (5^2 * 2.0928) = 3.4792e12
    logged ratio:  3.4792e12   (exact match)

  sample 13: same innovation, variance=2.1094
    reconstructed: (1.3492e7)^2 / (25 * 2.1094) = 3.4519e12
    logged ratio:  3.4519e12   (exact match)

The gate_size = 5 matches PX4's default EKF2_GPS_P_GATE.

Implication: the ratio is a squared quantity with a nonlinear dependence
on innovation variance. A small variance (typical) with a moderately
large innovation (5-10 sigma) gives a ratio in the 10^6 to 10^12 range.
These are not corrupted values. They are what PX4 computes internally
when deciding to reject a measurement.

This means the log+clip transform is a reasonable stability fix, but
the deeper story is that the ratio is a squared statistic. Papers
analyzing it should be aware that the tail is heavy by construction.
