Q5a: Why was the first dropout measure tautological?

The first dropout proxy was:

    dropout_frac = (expected_count - actual_count) / expected_count

where:

    expected_count = rate_hz * span_seconds
    rate_hz = (actual_count - 1) / span_seconds

Substituting:

    expected_count = ((actual_count - 1) / span_seconds) * span_seconds
                   = actual_count - 1

So:

    (expected_count - actual_count) = (actual_count - 1) - actual_count
                                    = -1

    dropout_frac = -1 / (actual_count - 1)   (always negative, tiny)

or with the +1 rounding, always zero. The fraction carried no
information about real dropouts. It was circular: rate was derived
from sample count, then compared back to sample count.

The v2 proxy replaces this by looking at the timestamp series
directly. A dropout is a gap in the timestamps. Specifically:

    gap_threshold = 10 * median(diff(timestamps))
    dropout_time  = sum of gaps larger than threshold
    dropout_frac  = dropout_time / total_span

This is a physical measure of missing recording time, independent
of the sample count. It is the measure used in the datasheet.

Q5b: Which dropout measure goes in the datasheet?

The datasheet uses the v2 measure:

    dropout_frac = fraction of total recording time spent in
                   timestamp gaps larger than 10 times the median
                   inter-sample interval

Computed per log, taking the maximum across four topics:
    vehicle_attitude
    vehicle_local_position
    sensor_combined
    estimator_innovation_test_ratios

Rationale for the choice:

  1. The .ulg file is discarded after streaming. pyulog's native
     dropout record (ulog.dropouts) cannot be recovered from the
     Parquet output. We chose the v2 measure because it works on
     the data we retain.

  2. The four topics span the range of publishing rates in the
     corpus. Attitude and IMU at 20-250 Hz, position at 5-125 Hz,
     ratios at 1.5-125 Hz. The gap threshold adapts automatically
     to each topic's rate.

  3. The measure is conservative. A 10x median gap on a 20 Hz
     topic is 0.5 seconds. On a 2 Hz topic it is 5 seconds. These
     are long, unambiguous recording breaks rather than noise.

Limitations of the measure:

  - Does not capture slow jitter or intermittent drops that are
    smaller than 10x the median gap.
  - For topics with irregular publish rates (e.g., sensor_combined
    during low-rate flights) the median may be dominated by a
    different sub-mode.
  - Values are absolute fractions. A 0.02 threshold means 2% of
    total recording time was lost.

What the datasheet should state:

    "dropout_frac is the fraction of total recording time spent
    in timestamp gaps larger than 10x the median inter-sample
    interval, computed as the maximum across vehicle_attitude,
    vehicle_local_position, sensor_combined and
    estimator_innovation_test_ratios. It is a proxy for the
    pyulog dropout record, which cannot be recovered from the
    Parquet output."

Q5d: Why did Track A move after the log transform?

Sir's observation: log(1+x) is a monotone transform. It cannot
change a score that uses a quantile. So why did Track A move?

Sir is correct at the per-ratio level. For a single ratio:

    p95(log(1+x)) = log(1 + p95(x))

The ordering is preserved. Coverage at the same quantile is
identical.

But the Track A composite score is not a per-ratio quantile. In
coverage_power_curve.py, Track A is computed as:

    Xn = (X - median_A) / IQR_A      # per-ratio normalization
    score_A = max(Xn, axis=1)        # max across 10 ratios

where median_A and IQR_A are computed on Group A.

Three effects cause the movement:

  1. The median and IQR change when the underlying values are
     log-transformed. For example, gps_hpos[0] has a heavy tail.
     Its IQR is dominated by the tail. After log transform, the
     tail is compressed and the IQR shrinks. Different ratios
     end up with different normalization factors.

  2. The max across ratios picks different winning ratios when
     the per-ratio normalizations shift. On a given flight, the
     ratio with the largest normalized value may be ratio A
     before the transform and ratio B after.

  3. The analysis set also changed between the two runs. The
     first Track A (0.0957 detection) ran on the 1,566-log set
     with the leaky clip. The second Track A (0.0426) ran on
     the 1,627-log set with the corrected clip.

So the movement is not caused by the log transform alone. It is
the interaction of the log transform with the IQR normalization
and the max-across-ratios step, plus the change in the analysis
set.

What this means:

  - Track A's per-ratio quantile coverage is log-invariant. The
    per-ratio coverage results (Section TABLE 4) are unaffected
    by the log transform.
  - Track A's composite detection power depends on the IQR
    normalization, which is not log-invariant. The detection
    power number for Track A should be reported with this
    caveat.

For the paper: report per-ratio coverage as the primary Track A
result. Report the composite detection power as secondary, with
the note that it depends on the normalization choice.
